## Design Context

### Users
Twitter/X followers, recruiters, coworkers, and engineering peers. Primary use case is discovery — someone wants to know more about Eric's career, typically around hiring decisions. The site needs to make the case quickly and convincingly: "I'd hire this person in a heartbeat."

### Brand Personality
Professional but fun. Deep care about craft and nuance. Three words: **crafted, confident, approachable**.

Not cold or corporate. Playful through tangible, carefully constructed objects. The sweet spot is someone who clearly sweats the details but doesn't take themselves too seriously.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme**: Currently exploring a dark charcoal studio with bright text and saturated LEGO colors. Use Lea Verou's two-state theme toggle: follow the OS by default, persist a literal override when choosing the opposite theme, clear it only when the user selects the OS's current theme, and retain it across OS changes. Resolve before first paint. The integrated homepage keeps the existing Home / Blog / Projects / Speaking / Uses navigation, sun/moon icon toggle, and subtle textured background. Use soft ground shadows and a slightly downward view onto each section floor; no SVG room backdrop.
- **References**: Linear.app (precision, restraint, dark-mode craft, smooth interactions), Clerk.com (polished developer-facing product, confident typography, clean hierarchy).
- **Anti-references**: The old cyan-gradient hero version of the site. Generic portfolio templates. Overly playful/illustrated personal sites. Corporate enterprise aesthetics.
- **Current direction**: Keep the existing Astro homepage as the foundation, including its avatar, biography, social links, Open Source, Recent Writing, and Archive. Replace the title and Experience artwork with interactive LEGO sculptures. Place the LEGO name across the top of the hero. Below it, place the avatar and social icons in the left column and the biography in the right column, preserving existing element sizes. Space the social icons evenly across exactly the avatar width. Stack these groups on narrow screens. The hero shares one physics area: the photo, social icons, and visible bio text lines are colliders; omit the “ec.” wordmark and “Still building” section. Eric's name has no trailing period and is a bright LEGO-yellow brick sculpture, using custom geometric letterforms drawn directly on the tile grid. Avoid recognizable arcade fonts such as Press Start 2P. Use the selected Curved bold lettering: heavier strokes, square capital E, and true curved LDraw tiles. Remove the lettering selector, hero instructions, redundant current-company link, and fixed bottom toolbar. Label the career section simply Experience, without a slogan or explanatory tagline. Only the sculpture in the currently hovered hero or career row pitches and yaws toward the cursor relative to its own center; other sculptures ease back to flat. only the center of its own page-local footprint produces its flat pose. Yaw follows horizontal pointer displacement freely. Pitch is flat at the sculpture center and below; above its center it tips backward toward the top of the screen, limited to a gentle 12 degrees. Never pitch forward/downward. Use neutral studio lighting and preserve saturated plastic colors without a brown or washed-out cast. Career history follows below the fold, with a brick-built company logo beside each entry. TestDriver uses its helmet without its wordmark.
- **Construction**: Use a considered mix of standard brick and plate sizes (1×4, 2×2, 2×4, etc.), nearly seamless joins, smooth studless tops, restrained bevels, and a restrained ABS material. Use official LDraw studless tile geometry with underside connections and full brick-like body depth (3× tile depth) so rotated sidewalls read clearly. Use glossy dielectric plastic with clearcoat and visible softbox reflections. Build rounded name corners from nested official quarter-circle tiles (27507, 79393, 27925, 25269), with a matching smooth inset and compound curved collision shapes. Seams should be very faint; avoid dark outlines or visible top studs. Favor readable brick proportions over excess miniature pieces; do not double every sculpture with a hidden physical backing layer. Fine silhouette detail must coexist with readable, substantial pieces. Avoid uniform voxel or Minecraft-like construction.
- **Interaction**: Click-and-drag sweeps continuously through nearby bricks with a soft radius of influence; the gesture's direction and speed push the pieces. A stationary click blasts a local cluster. Holding builds a localized rumble and increases the blast radius and force on release; moving into a sweep cancels the charge. Freeze the current sculpture rotation while pressing or dragging. Suppress visual rumble for reduced motion. Sound begins only after a deliberate interaction: charging is silent, retaining only the visual rumble. Only section-floor collisions make a quiet sound, using short Raycast × NuPhy keyboard demo excerpts. Brick-to-brick, wall, and text collisions are silent; avoid metallic ringing and generated clack loops. Blast release uses the selected Pixabay Cork sample by freesound_community, credited in the page. Limit overlapping sounds and ignore resting jitter. Keep a small sound icon beside the Experience heading; mute when the page is hidden. Rebuild moves each piece directly from its current pose into its live target position and orientation, using an accelerating quartic ease-in that snaps into the slot without slowing down. Do not record or replay historical fall paths. The same sweeping gesture stirs loose bricks. Detached pieces fall to a floor below the complete hero or each career row, and the pile scrolls with that section. All physics floors, side boundaries, and pile touch targets span the full viewport width, independently of the narrower content column. Clicking a completely destroyed sculpture's pile or empty logo area rebuilds that section independently. Keyboard activation of the empty sculpture rebuilds it too. Visible career text lines act as static physical ledges, so bricks bounce off headings, dates, and paragraph lines. Build colliders from actual wrapped text ranges and align their depth with the camera projection; leave inter-line whitespace open, preserve viewport walls and floors, and never make the hidden name fallback collide with its own bricks. An invisible ceiling follows the top of the viewport while floors remain attached to their sections. Expand row canvases upward only as far as flying pieces require, retaining native scroll anchoring and trimming excess canvas space after settling. Pieces must settle on floors, text, and other bricks; damp late contact jitter and stop physics entirely when idle. Give each deliberate launch or sweep a settling budget based on twice its estimated ballistic flight plus a cushion, bounded to 2.5–6 seconds of visible simulation time. Latch settled pieces as static colliders so contact chatter cannot wake them; deliberate gestures restore their dynamic mass and reset the budget. Offscreen time does not consume the budget. Keep physics to one fixed solver step per frame to avoid catch-up spikes, and recalculate mass only when a settled piece becomes dynamic, never repeatedly during an active sweep. Remove offscreen rows and their boundaries from the solver, preserving their poses for revisits. Deliberate blasts and sweeps wake sleeping pieces. Each sculpture uses a full-viewport-width canvas anchored in its own row (the name in its shared hero), so the browser scrolls artwork and text together. A shared offscreen renderer updates row canvases only when their artwork changes. Sculptures and debris must not be clipped by narrow logo containers. Rasterize the opaque union of each sculpture into a single silhouette before blurring and projecting onto its section floor. Shadows must be clearly visible soft gray, lighter than text, like a large overhead softbox. Balance broad blur with enough opacity to anchor the sculptures; avoid both dark bands and barely visible haze. Keep the footprint centered horizontally beneath the sculpture, with a subtle soft contact halo for grounded bricks. Missing bricks reveal one continuous silhouette with a shallow socket about 5% darker than the backdrop, a tight 1–1.5px inner shadow, and a faint hairline rim; never show individual tile outlines in the skeleton. The silhouette rotates and lifts with the sculpture, immediately behind the tile backs. Use a faint open-hand cursor shadow aligned with the same ground projection; flatten the hand before applying blur so the shadow stays soft in both directions, and render only visible sculptures. Include rebuild and keyboard controls, and respect reduced motion for ambient movement.

### Design Principles
1. **Craft is the message** — Every detail signals engineering quality. Spacing, typography, and interactions should feel considered, not generated. The site itself is a portfolio piece.
2. **Confident restraint** — Bold where it matters, quiet everywhere else. Like Linear: the confidence comes from what's *not* there as much as what is.
3. **A studio for physical objects** — Use an off-white backdrop, dimensional lighting, gentle tilt, and restrained motion to make the builds feel tangible.
4. **Hire-in-a-heartbeat fast** — Visitors are evaluating. Respect their time. Clear hierarchy, scannable content, no friction between landing and understanding who Eric is.
5. **Crafted play** — Personality comes through recognizable, deliberately assembled LEGO objects and satisfying interactions. Protect readable career content and navigation while visitors play.
